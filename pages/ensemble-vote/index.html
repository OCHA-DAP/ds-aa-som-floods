The ensemble vote level

A trigger point votes when the ensemble median crosses its level. The median is a choice: the vote could instead require 30%, 60% or 90% of the members. This note tests every vote level the GloFAS v4 reforecast can resolve, on all four windows and both legs, to see whether any of them performs better than the median. September 2026.

The setup

Each vote level k means: a point votes on a day when at least k of the 11 reforecast members put that day over the point's level, taking the most alarming issue per valid day, the same convention as the operational backtest. Everything else stays at the adopted rules: same points, same same-day vote counts, same season months, and levels fitted on the v4 reanalysis at each leg's return period. The median rule is k = 6: the median crosses when at least half the members do, and half of 11 rounds up to 6. Each level is scored against the two-gauge benchmark on severe years (1-in-5), 2003–2023.

The sweep

What the sweep shows.
  • No level catches an extra severe year. Detection does not change with k on any leg of any window: even one member of eleven, at the required number of points on the same day, does not reach the levels in the missed years. Those misses come from the model's magnitudes, and no vote level can recover them.
  • At leads 1–7 the level barely matters. The ensemble is tight at short leads: most members cross together or not at all. The one exception is Juba Gu, where levels above the median drop the 2016 activation, a flood year but not a severe one on that window.
  • At leads 7–12 the level matters, in both directions. The spread is wider there. Above the median, Juba Deyr loses its 2015 activation, the one outside the benchmark, but Shabelle Deyr loses 2014, a severe year. Below the median, Juba Gu adds 2010 and 2016 without catching any additional severe year.

What about Google for Gu?

The Google GRRR reforecast is a single trace per issue and lead: the archive has no ensemble members, so there is no member share to choose. The Gu legs vote when the forecast value crosses the level, which is what the operational check on the trigger page replays. The live Flood Hub display does show uncertainty bounds, but they are not in the public archive; with API access they could be logged from now on, and this question asked of Google once a few seasons accumulate. Until then the Gu windows are monitored on GloFAS as a labelled stand-in, where the member share applies as above.

What this means for monitoring

Keep the median. No level performs better on any leg, and the one improvement on offer, a stricter Juba Deyr readiness, costs a severe year on Shabelle Deyr readiness. One vote level for the whole mechanism is also easier to defend than four tuned ones. The daily monitoring dashboard shows each point's member share over its level, so the vote can be read straight off the bars against the 50% mark.

Caveats. The reforecast has 11 members, so vote levels can only be tested in steps of 9%; the operational forecast has 50 members and reads finer, but the flatness at short leads means the finer scale has little to add. These scores use the full 2003–2023 archive, so they differ slightly from the trigger page's swap heatmap, which scores only the years Google's archive also covers. The readiness rows use reanalysis-fitted levels so that every vote level is compared within one design; the adopted readiness leg refits its levels on the 7-to-12-day forecast series itself, so its activation years differ from the mechanism table. And each window has 3 to 5 severe events: every difference on this page is a one-or-two-event difference.
