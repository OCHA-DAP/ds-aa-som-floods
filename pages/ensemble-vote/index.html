The ensemble vote level: median or a probability?

A trigger point votes when the ensemble median crosses its level. The median is one setting on a dial: a point could instead vote when 30%, 50% or 80% of members cross. This note sweeps that dial across every window and both legs on the only ensemble lead-time archive that exists, and checks whether any setting beats the median. September 2026.

The setup

Each vote level k means: a point votes on a day when at least k of the 11 reforecast members put that day over the point's level, taking the most alarming issue per valid day, the same convention as the operational backtest. Everything else is held at the adopted rules: same points, same same-day vote counts, same season months, and levels fitted on the v4 reanalysis at each leg's return period, so the comparison is model-consistent throughout. The median rule is exactly k = 6: the median crosses when at least half the members do, and half of 11 rounds up to 6. Each setting is scored against the two-gauge benchmark on severe years (1-in-5), 2003–2023.

The sweep

What moves, and what does not.
  • No level catches an extra severe year, anywhere. Detection is flat in k on every leg of every window: even one member of eleven, at the required number of points on the same day, does not reach the levels in the missed years. The misses are the model's magnitude problem, not a vote-statistic problem, and no probability setting can recover them.
  • Action leg: near-total indifference. At leads 1–7 the ensemble is tight; either most members cross or almost none do. The one mover is Juba Gu, where levels above the median drop the 2016 activation, a flood year but not a severe one on that window.
  • Readiness leg: the dial moves both ways. At leads 7–12 the spread is wider. Stricter than the median removes Juba Deyr's 2015 activation, the one outside the benchmark, but the same strictness on Shabelle Deyr loses 2014, a severe year. More lenient than the median adds activations (Juba Gu picks up 2010 and 2016) without catching a single additional severe year.

What about Google for Gu?

The dial does not exist there. The Google GRRR reforecast is a single trace per issue and lead: the archive has no ensemble members, so there is no member share to tune, and the Gu legs vote when the forecast value itself crosses the level. That is exactly what the operational check on the trigger page replays. The live Flood Hub display carries uncertainty bounds, but they are not in the public archive; if API access materialises, logging them forward would let this question be asked of Google too. Until then the Gu windows are monitored on GloFAS as a labelled stand-in, where the member share applies as above.

What this means for monitoring

Keep the median. It sits on the flat optimum of every leg of every window, and the one-event improvements available elsewhere (a stricter Juba Deyr readiness) are paid for by a one-event loss on another window (a severe year on Shabelle Deyr readiness). A single vote level for the whole mechanism is simpler to state and to defend, and 50% of members is that level. The daily monitoring dashboard already shows each point's member share over its level, so the vote is readable directly against the 50% mark.

Caveats. The reforecast has 11 members, so the sweep resolves the dial only in steps of 9%; the operational forecast has 50 members and reads finer, but the short-lead flatness leaves that granularity little to exploit. These scores use the full 2003–2023 archive, so they differ slightly from the trigger page's swap heatmap, which scores only the years Google's archive also covers. The readiness rows use reanalysis-fitted levels so every setting is compared within one design; the adopted readiness leg refits its levels on the 7-to-12-day forecast series itself, so its absolute activation years differ from the mechanism table. And each window has 3 to 5 severe events: every difference on this page is a one-or-two-event difference.
